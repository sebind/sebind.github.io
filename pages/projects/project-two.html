Project

Design and Development of Functional Materials for Hydrogen Storage and Carbon Capture via First-Principles Density Functional Theory and Integrated machine learning approaches

Computational Materials Science, DFT

Overview

Ref. No. CRS/2024-25/04/1602
Hydrogen storage and carbon capture are two critical research areas for sustainable solutions to growing energy demands and climate change. While solid-state hydrogen storage is the safest and most effective way for future hydrogen-fuel technologies, capturing carbon compounds directly from industrial emissions is crucial in reducing carbon footprint and regulating climate change. Density functional theory has transformed materials research in terms of design and modeling to an in-depth atomistic understanding of material behavior focused on specific applications. DFT can be a powerful tool in identifying potential materials that can adsorb hydrogen and carbon surpassing the need for many experimental trials with material wastage and energy. These simulations and predictive modelling-based investigations can significantly contribute to the National Green Hydrogen Mission, as well as the Carbon Capture, Utilization and Storage (CCUS) policies of the country and achieving the proposed milestones, such as 5 million metric tons of H2/year and 45% less carbon emission by 2030.

Research

1. To compute the adsorption/desorption properties of various material systems using DFT methods for hydrogen storage and carbon capture.
2. To collect the data from literature and libraries to use as training datasets.
3. To analyze the thermodynamic stability and performance of functionalized/composite materials and nanostructures.
4. To use the simulation data and available data sets to train models for the accelerated prediction of novel materials for hydrogen production and carbon capture.
5. To identify potential candidates using these models and synthesize them for validation.
